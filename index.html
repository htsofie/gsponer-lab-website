Reading cell signalling and quality control from the data

The Gsponer Lab builds computational tools that combine genomic, transcriptomic and proteomic data to study how cells signal and how they keep their proteins in working order. We focus on intrinsically disordered proteins, protein misfolding and aggregation, and what goes wrong in cancer and neurodegenerative disease.

    Research

    Research

    Advances in high-throughput biology produce large amounts of pan-omic data. We build the computational methods to turn that data into a systems-level picture of cellular processes.

    Theme

    Signalling protein structure and function

    Intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs) lack a stable three-dimensional structure on their own. They sit at the centre of signalling and regulatory cascades in eukaryotic cells, and defects in their interactions are linked to disease.

    We develop computational methods to:

    • identify the structures of signalling protein complexes,
    • assess how IDPs fit into signalling networks,
    • determine the structural constraints on protein evolution and regulation.
    Theme

    Protein misfolding and aggregation

    We study protein aggregation in neurodegenerative disease by computational means. This includes predicting the structures proteins adopt in amyloid fibrils.

    We also use high-throughput screening to understand how protein interactions are disrupted in diseases such as Huntington's and Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease, and which signalling pathways are affected.
