Computational biology · Vancouver

Protein design, computational biology, protein biophysics

The Gsponer Lab uses gen-AI, machine learning, computational biology and statistical analysis to analyse protein structure and function across a range of landscapes. The lab's main projects are designing protein binders that bind specific targets, building affinity and interaction prediction methods, and researching the role of phosphorylation as a driver of protein–protein interactions and protein switches.

    Research

    Research

    We use gen-AI, machine learning, computational biology and statistical analysis to analyse protein structure and function.

    Project

    Protein binder design

    We design proteins that bind specific targets. Generative models, including diffusion and flow matching, build a binder backbone against a target. Sequence design and structure and affinity prediction then filter the candidates.

    Project

    Affinity and interaction prediction

    We build methods that predict whether two proteins interact, how strongly, and through which interface. This includes interfaces that involve disordered regions and conformational change, where current structure predictors struggle.

    Project

    Phosphorylation, interactions and protein switches

    We study how phosphorylation drives protein–protein interactions and acts as a switch that turns binding on or off, and we use that understanding to design protein switches.
